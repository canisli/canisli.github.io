4. Symmetries

The plane has translation, bilateral (reflection), rotational, glide, and rotoreflection symmetries.

4.1. Orthogonal group and SO(3)

  • Def 𝑂𝑛 is the group of 𝑛×𝑛 orthogonal matrices. SO𝑛 is the normal subgroup of 𝑂𝑛 containing orientation-preserving transformations (det𝐴=1).
  • Lem If 𝐴∈𝑂𝑛 preserves subspace 𝐸, then 𝐴 preserves 𝐸⟂as well.
  • Prop 𝑂𝑛 is generated by reflections.
  • Some lemmas regarding eigenvalues:
    • Lem Every eigenvalue of an orthogonal matrix lies on the unit circle.
    • Lem If 𝐴∈𝑂𝑛, then 𝜆 is an eigenvalue of 𝐴 implies 𝜆−1 is as well.
  • Thm Every 𝐴∈𝑂𝑛 admits an orthonormal basis where it 𝐴 is block diagonal, made up of 1s, −1s, and 2D rotation blocks. This form is unique up to permutation of blocks.
  • Thm (Euler) SO3 is the set of 3D rotation matrices.

4.2. Isometries

  • Def An isometry is a mapping ℝ𝑛→ℝ𝑛, not necessarily linear, that preserves distances.
  • Thm4 Suppose 𝐴:ℝ𝑛→ℝ𝑛 is an isometry that fixes the origin. Then 𝐴 is linear, i.e. 𝐴∈𝑂𝑛.

4.2.1. Isometries on the plane

  • Prop Classification of isometries on ℝ2
    1. Every orientation-preserving isometry of ℝ2 is either a translation or rotation about some point
    2. Every orientation-reversing isometry of ℝ2 is a glide5 or a reflection.

4.4. Discrete subgroups of ℝ𝑛 are lattices.

  • Def A subgroup of ℝ𝑛 is discrete if every point is isolated. It suffices to just check whether zero is isolated. If the subgroup is not discrete, then it is dense in ℝ𝑛.
  • Thm6 Let 𝐻 be a discrete subgroup of ℝ𝑛. Then 𝐻 is generated over ℤ by 𝑟≤𝑛 vectors which are linearly independent over ℝ𝑛.

4.5. Discrete subgroups of 𝑀2 (Frieze and Wallpaper groups)

  • Def Let 𝐺 be a subgroup of 𝑀𝑛.
    • The translation group 𝐿 is 𝐺∩ℝ𝑛.
    • The point group 𝐺‾ is the image of 𝜋:𝑀𝑛→𝑂𝑛 applied on 𝐺.
      • By the first isomorphism theorem, 𝐺/ker𝜑≅im𝜑, i.e. 𝐺/𝐿≅𝐺‾
  • Def A subgroup of 𝑀𝑛 is discrete if
    1. It’s translation group 𝐿 is discrete.
    2. The point group of 𝐺‾ is finite7, i.e. 𝐺‾=𝐶𝑛 or 𝐷𝑛.
  • Lem (Obvious) Suppose 𝐴∈𝑂𝑛. Then 𝑡𝑣𝜑=𝜑𝑡𝑣′ where 𝑣′=𝜑(𝑣)
