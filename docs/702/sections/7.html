7. Galois Theory

Recall an 𝐹-homomorphism preserves roots in 𝐹 of a polynomial in 𝐹[𝑥]. It can be viewed as a ring homomorphism. Since a field has only {0} and itself as ideals, nontrivial homomorphisms are injective.

Let 𝐾/𝐹 be a field extension of 𝐹. Let 𝜎:𝐾→𝐾 be a 𝐹-automorphism. Then it’s easy to check that 𝜎 permutes the roots of polynomials in 𝐹[𝑥]. For example, the ℝ-automorphisms of ℂ are identity and conjugation.

7.7. The Main Theorem

Theorem 41 (Main Theorem). Let 𝐾 be a Galois extension of a field 𝐹, and let 𝐺 be its Galois group. There is a bijective correspondence between subgroups of 𝐺 and intermediate groups 𝐹⊂𝐿⊂𝐾.

Forward direction: Send 𝐻⊂𝐺 to its fixed field 𝐹⊂𝐾𝐻⊂𝐾.
Reverse direction: Send 𝐹⊂𝐿⊂𝐾 to its Galois group 𝐺(𝐾/𝐿)⊂𝐺(𝐾/𝐹).

Proof

It suffices to show that composing the two maps in either order is the identity map. By the Lemma 41 (corollary of Fixed Field Theorem), 𝐺(𝐾/𝐾𝐻)=𝐻, proving the forward direction. The backward direction is proved by the corollary of Theorem 39.

The two sets have the same size if and only if the extension is Galois by definition.

7.8. Cubic Equations

Let 𝑓(𝑥)∈𝐹[𝑥] be an irreducible cubic polynomial, and 𝐾=𝐹(𝛼1,𝛼2,𝛼3) be a splitting field (Galois extension) of 𝑓 over 𝐹. By Vieta’s theorem, the sum of the roots is in 𝐹 so the third root is generated by the first two:

𝐹⊂𝐹(𝛼1)⊂𝐹(𝛼1,𝛼2)=𝐹(𝛼1,𝛼2,𝛼3)=𝐾. (55)

We have [𝐹(𝛼1):𝐹]=3 since 𝑓 is irreducible. We have [𝐹(𝛼1,𝛼2):𝐹(𝛼1)] equals 1 or 2 depending whether the polynomial 𝑞(𝑥)=𝑓(𝑥)/(𝑥−𝛼1)is irreducible.

The Galois group is a transitive subgroup of 𝑆3 and thus it is either 𝐴3=𝐶3 or 𝑆3. We want to check whether 𝑞(𝑥) is irreducible in 𝐹(𝛼1)[𝑥]. We’d rather work in the field 𝐹. The next theorem allows this.

Let 𝛿=𝐷=(𝛼1−𝛼2)(𝛼1−𝛼3)(𝛼2−𝛼3). Note that 𝛿∈𝐾, 𝛿≠0, and swapping two roots mulitlpies 𝛿 by −1.

Theorem 43 (Galois Theory for Cubics). If 𝐷 is a square in 𝐹 (i.e. 𝛿∈𝐹), then [𝐾:𝐹]=3. Otherwise [𝐾:𝐹]=6.
Proof
𝛿∈𝐹 means that it is fixed by every element of 𝐺 (𝐹-automorphism), meaning that 𝐺 cannot have any odd permutations. Otherwise, 𝐺 contains an odd permutation since only 𝐹 is fixed by 𝐺.
