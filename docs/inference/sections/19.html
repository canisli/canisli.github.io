18. Conjugate Priors

So far:

  • Least informative prior chooses weights over models such that observing 𝑦 is maximally informative about which model 𝑥 generated it.
    • The name is ironic: it refers to the fact that it’s hardest for nature to exploit against you in the minimax game, and in the Bayesian sense it’s the most informative prior.
  • It’s hard to compute, and we expoit the flexibility of weights to compute the maximally ignorant prior. But this may not be easy to update with new beliefs.
  • Hence we develop conjugate priors.

It says that as N → ∞, the posterior distribution converges to a Gaussian centered at the true parameter with variance equal to 1/(N · Fisher information), regardless of the prior (provided the prior has support at the true value). So asymptotically, two different priors give you the same posterior — the data completely washes out the prior’s influence.

  • And then in the small data regime, you choose least informative / maximally ignorant prior.

18.3. Conjugate Prior Families

Continue to restrict our attention to the case of conditionally i.i.d. models, whereby

𝑝𝘆|𝘹(𝐲|𝑥)=∏𝑖=1𝑁𝑝𝘺|𝘹(𝑦𝑖|𝑥) (18.4)

Let’s develop some notation. Let 𝑝(⋅) denote a belief before revision, and 𝑞(⋅;𝐭1(𝑦1))=𝑇𝑦1[𝑝] after revision based on data 𝘺1=𝑦1, where 𝐭1(𝑦1) is a sufficient statistic22 of the data.

From this perspective, we see that 𝑇𝑦1 maps a single distribution 𝑝=𝑞0(⋅;𝛉0) in a family 𝑄0 to one in a family 𝑄1 of distributions 𝑞1(⋅;𝛉1) where 𝛉1=(𝐭1(𝑦1),𝛉0). Upon receipt of subsequent data 𝘺2=𝑦2, the particular member of 𝑄1 is mapped to 𝑞2(⋅;𝛉2) where 𝛉2=(𝐭2(𝑦1,𝑦2),𝛉0) is a sufficient statistic of the now expanded data. We are interested in the case when there exists a single continuously parametirezd family 𝑄 such that 𝑄1=𝑄2=…=𝑄.

Definition 18.3 (Conjugate Prior Family). Let

𝑄={𝑞(⋅;𝛉)∈𝒫︀𝒳︀:𝛉∈𝜗⊂ℝ𝐾} (18.5)

denote a family of distributions specified by some dimension 𝐾, region 𝜗⊂ℝ𝐾, and parameterized for 𝑞, where the mapping from 𝜗 to Q is continuous and continuously invertible. Then 𝑄 is a conjugate prior family f for the model Equation 18.4 if for every 𝑦∈𝒴︀, we have 𝑝𝘹|𝘺(⋅|𝑦)∈𝑄 whenever 𝑝𝘹(⋅)∈𝑄.

As notation, if 𝛉0∈𝜗 denotes the parameter specifying 𝑝𝑥∈𝑄, then the parameter specifying 𝑝𝘹|𝘺(⋅|𝑦) based on an observatoin 𝑦 we denote using the function 𝛉(𝑦;𝛉0).

𝛉0 is often referred to as a hyperparameter or metaparameter, representing the effect of “pseudo-observations” arising out of domain knowledge. For example, starting with a Beta distribution, the update rule turns out to be that each observation either increments 𝛼 (if 𝑦=1) or increments 𝛽 (if y = 0). So any Beta(α,β) prior can be produced by starting somewhere and processing fictious data. I.e. 𝛼=3,𝛽=7 is worth about 10 pseudo-observations, biased towards zero, and as 𝑁→∞, the real data domiantes and you recover MLE.

18.5. Conjugate Prior Families for Exponential Families

We know that a conjugate prior family can exist only for an exponential family.

Given an exponential family 𝑝𝘆|𝘅(𝐲|𝐱)=exp{𝛌(𝐱)𝖳𝐭(𝐲)−𝛼(𝐱)+𝛽(𝐲)}, it is straightforward to verify that

𝑄={𝑞(⋅;𝐭,𝑁):𝑞(𝐱;𝐭,𝑁)=exp{[𝐭𝖳𝛌(𝐱)−𝑁𝛼(𝐱)]−𝛾(𝐭,𝑁)}} (18.7)
where 𝛾(𝐭,𝑁) is a normalization constant, is a conjugate prior family.

Properties

  • This is also a natural exponential family (see definition above).
  • This is itself a canonical exponential family with natural statistic (𝛌(𝑥)−𝛼(𝐱))𝖳.
  • The same holds for discrete observations: conjugate prior families exist for exponential families.
