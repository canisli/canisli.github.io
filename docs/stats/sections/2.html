2. Frequentist Inference

Three goals of inference:

  1. Estimation
  2. Confidence Intervals
  3. Hypothesis Tests

These are all procedures: given that data we compute a statistic and infer something.

Definition 6. Let 𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛 be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with common distribution ℙ.

A statistical model is a pair (𝐸,{𝑃𝜃,𝜃∈Θ}) where

  • 𝐸⊂ℝ is the sample space,
  • ℙ is the family of probability distributions on 𝐸, and
  • Θ is the parameter set.

We assume the statistical model is specified, i.e. ℙ=ℙ𝜃 for some 𝜃∈Θ.

Given a statistical model and observed data, the likelihood function expresses how plausiable each 𝜃 makes the observed data:

𝐿(𝜃;𝑥)=𝑝(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛|𝜃). (30)

This is the same definition that Bayesians use, except they care about the posterior: 𝑝(𝜃|𝑥)=𝐿(𝜃;𝑥)𝑝(𝜃).

Theorem 7 (Cramer–Rao Bound). For any unbiased estimator 𝜃̂, under regularity conditions

Var(𝜃̂)≥1𝐼(𝜃true). (42)

In multiple dimensions, we have

Var(𝜃̂)⪰[𝐼(𝜃true)]−1, (43)

tr(Var(𝜃̂))≥tr([𝐼(𝜃true)]−1). (44)

Intuitively, the Fisher information is related to the curvature of the likelihood function near 𝜃true, and thus sharper peaks lead to more precision.

Definition 10. We say an unbiased estimator is efficient if it saturates the CR bound. Biased estimators by definition cannot be efficient.

A more general definition of efficiency that applies for consistent estimators is the variance of an estimator relative to the CR bound to another estimator, since usually the estimators are consistent and asymptotically unbiased. The relative efficiency is then the ratio of their variances.

We can beat the CR bound if regularity conditions aren’t satisfied or with unbiased estimators (for example: James-Stein estimator for the mean).

2.2. Robust Estimators

For i.i.d. 𝒩︀(0,1), the variance of the mean is 1𝑛. The variance of the median is 𝜋2𝑛 ([TODO] how? median is computed using empirical cdf). The ratio is 2𝜋, and we say the sample median is 64% efficienct compared to the mean (only uses 64% of the information compared to the mean under Gaussian assumptions, Definition 10).

But under heavy-tailed data, the mean’s variance can blow up and the median becomes more efficient.

We can assess robustness as a function of how misspecified our model is. One metric is the breakdown point: fraction of contaminated data needed to drive the estimator arbitrarily wrong. Mean is 0%. Median is 50%. Another is sensitivity to small outliers.

Generally we have an efficiency-robustness tradeoff. Robust procedures often have lower efficiency under the ideal model, but they degrade more gracefully under misspecification.

2.4. Hypothesis Tests

Like confidence intervals, hypothesis tests are a procedure that, in the long run, makes only a controlled fraction of mistakes (𝛼 and 1−𝛽).

Assume the model is well-specified and split the parameter space into disjoint, complementary subsets defined by 𝐻0 and 𝐻1.

Definition 12. A hypothesis test at level 𝛼 is a random decision rule 𝜑(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛) that outputs 0 or 1.

  • 𝜑=0: “fail to reject 𝐻0”
  • 𝜑=1: “reject 𝐻0”

If 𝐻0 is true, the procedure satisfies

sup𝜃∈𝐻0𝑃𝜃(𝜑(𝑋)=1)≤𝛼. (46)

This means that no matter which 𝜃 in the null region is the true one, the probability of false rejection never exceeds 𝛼.

The power of the test is 𝜋(𝜃)=𝑃𝜃(𝜑(𝑋)=1). The Type II error is 𝛽(𝜃)=1−𝜋(𝜃) when 𝜃∈𝐻1.

The coverage is only for false rejection (Type I error). We don’t have a similar coverage guarantee for 𝛽. Ideally we design our test to have a small 𝛽.

Why not flip 𝐻0 and 𝐻1? By construction our test controls the false rejection rate. Hence we want to set 𝐻0 to be disease presence. False negative is way worse than false positive. Similiarly, in scientific discoveries, we set “no effect” as the null to avoid spurious discoveries.

2.5. Chi-Squared and KS Tests

The 𝜒2-distribution with 𝑘 degrees of freedom is the distribution of the sum of squares of 𝑘 independent standard normal random variables.

These pop up in tests used for

  1. Goodness-of-fit (does this dice look fair)
  2. Test of independence (are two categorical variables independent?)

2.5.1. Goodness of Fit

Say you have 𝑘 discrete outcomes or bins from a continuous distribution. Then for each bin, we should have 𝑂𝑖∼Binomial(𝑛,𝑝𝑖) with 𝔼[𝑂𝑖]=𝐸𝑖. In fact, the vector (𝑂1,…,𝑂𝑘) is sampled from a multinomial with Var𝑂𝑖=𝑛𝑝𝑖(1−𝑝𝑖) and Cov(𝑂𝑖,𝑂𝑗)=−𝑛𝑝𝑖𝑝𝑗 for 𝑖≠𝑗.2

Remarks:

When CLT doesn’t apply, we use Fisher’s exact test.

The binless continuous analog is the Kolmogorov–Smirnov / KS test, which answers

  1. Does a sample come from a fully specifide continuous cdf 𝐹0?
  2. Do two samples come from the same (unknown) continuous distribution?

The statistic is the largest vertical gap between their CDF curves which follows a Kolmogorov distribution. The vertical gap is a Brownian bridge (Brownian motion conditioned on that the process has the same value at 𝑡=0 and 𝑡=𝑇).

2.5.2. Independence Test

Suppose we have two categorical variables 𝑋 and 𝑌 with 𝑟 and 𝑐 levels respectively. Then we can apply a 𝜒2 gof test to the joint pdf to check for independence:

𝐻0:𝑃(𝑋=𝑖,𝑌=𝑗)=𝑃(𝑋=𝑖)𝑃(𝑌=𝑗) (52)

The twist is that we assume we don’t know the marginals in advance, so we estimate them from the data. This reduces the dof from 𝑟𝑐−1 to (𝑟−1)(𝑐−1). This pops out of the multivariate normal approximations but is hard to see. (Fisher and Pearson had a feud about it).

If we did know the hypothesized marginals, then we’d be back to an ordinary gof test with 𝑟𝑐−1 dof.

2.6. Resampling Methods (e.g. Bootstrapping)

Assessing estimators, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests require us to think about repeatedly sampling new datasets from the true distribution. Since we can’t do this, we instead try resampling from the data itself.

  1. Bootstrapping: Resample with replacement from our data, same sample size 𝑛. Compute statistic on each sample to get a distribution for 𝜃̂.
    • Motivation: Obviously this works for large 𝑛. Hence its worth trying in general.
  2. Jackknife: Systematically leave one observation out, recompute the statistic everytime.
    • Motivation: If your estimate is stable to leaving out one observation, then its variance is low.
  3. Permutation tests: If these two variables are unrelated, then we can randomly permute the labels and recompute the statistic each time to get a distribution under 𝐻0 and then compute a p-value.
