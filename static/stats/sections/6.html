6.1. Monte Carlo simulation and variance reduction (importance sampling, stratification)

  • Monte carlo integration ⇒ change of measure.

  • Importance sampling. Suppose we want to estimate a probability, say 𝑃(𝑌>4) if 𝑌∼𝒩︀(0,1) using an indicator variable 𝑋.

    𝔼[𝑋]=∫𝑥(𝑦)𝑝(𝑦)d𝑦=∫𝑥(𝑦)𝑝(𝑦)𝑞(𝑦)𝑞(𝑦)d𝑦 (87)
    By sampling in the region where 𝑥(𝑦)𝑝(𝑦) is large, we reduce the variance of 𝑥(𝑦)𝑝(𝑦)𝑞(𝑦).

  • For example, let’s compare naive Monte Carlo with importance sampling at 𝒩︀(4,1). In the naive case, we have Bernoulli trials:

    𝑝̂=1𝑛∑𝑖=1𝑛𝟙{𝑥>4} (88)

    with 𝔼[𝑝̂]=𝑝 and Var𝑝̂=𝑝(1−𝑝)𝑛. With importance sampling, we have

    𝑝̂=1𝑛∑𝑖=1𝑛𝟙{𝑥>4}𝑤(𝑥) (89)
    where 𝑤(𝑥)=𝑝(𝑥)𝑞(𝑥). For any 𝑛 we get a 7000 times reduction in the variance.
  • Another method is stratification: partition domain into disjoint strata. Within each strata, draw independent samples from the conditional distribution. Every region is sampled. In practice, we can apply importance sampling within each stratum.
  • Rejecting sampling: scale another distribution and pretend to throw darts.
