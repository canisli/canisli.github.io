4. Bayesian inference

Bayes’ theorem follows trivially from how we axiomatically define conditional probability. It introduces a philosophically different view of probability than frequentist.

Frequentist: 7/10 heads is not surprising IF its a fair coin.
Bayesian: Given a uniform prior and this data, its 88.7% probable that 𝜃≥0.5.

Frequentist: Add penalities to the negative log likelihood loss function and get MLE.
Bayesian: Add priors and get MAP estimate. MLE is just MAP with uniform prior.

Frequentist: Minimize risk Bayesian: Minimize posterior expected loss (posterior mean, median, mode/MAP depending on whether loss is squared, absolute, or 0-1 loss).

4.1. Credible interval

Given a prior 𝑝(𝜃) and a likelihood 𝑝(data|𝜃), we obtain the posterior 𝑝(𝜃|data).

Definition 16. A 100(1−𝛼)% credible interval for 𝜃 is a subset 𝐶 of the parameter space such that 𝑃(𝜃∈𝐶|data)=1−𝛼.

For equal tails case, we can simply take the 𝛼2 and 1−𝛼2 percentiles of the posteriors.

If 𝜃 is high dimensional or the posterior is nonanalytical, we use Markov chain Monte Carlo to sample the posterior and and estimate a credible interval.
