3. Semiclassical (WKB) Approximation

Define the local momentum as 𝑝(𝑥)=2𝑚(𝐸−𝑉(𝑥)), and the local de Broglie wavelength as 𝜆(𝑥)=ℎ/𝑝(𝑥). We assume a time-independent potential. The time-independent Schrodinger equation takes on a nice form:

𝑝̂2𝜓(𝑥)=𝑝2(𝑥)𝜓(𝑥) (17)

In general, the wavefunction can be written as an amplitude plus a phase

Ψ(𝐱,𝑡)=𝜌(𝐱,𝑡)exp(𝑖𝑆̃(𝐱,𝑡)/ℏ), (18)

for real functions 𝜌(𝐱,𝑡) and 𝑆(𝐱,𝑡), where 𝜌 represents the probability density, and 𝑆̃ is related to the probability current via 𝐽(𝐱,𝑡)=Im(ℎ𝑚Ψ∗∇Ψ)=Im(∇Ψ2+𝜌𝑖ℏ∇𝑆̃)ℏ𝑚=(𝜌∇𝑆̃)/𝑚.

Let 𝑆=𝑆̃−𝑖ℏ2ln(𝜌(𝑥,𝑡)), so that our ansatz looks much simpler:

Ψ(𝑥,𝑡)=exp(𝑖𝑆(𝑥,𝑡)/ℏ). (19)

This is still general, since 𝑆̃ and 𝜌 can be identified from the real and imaginary components of 𝑆, respectively.

3.1. Time-independent WKB

Suppose 𝑉 is time-independent, and consider using the above ansatz for the energy eigenstates, i.e. 𝜓(𝑥)=exp(𝑖𝑆(𝑥)/ℏ). Plugging this into the TISE (Equation 17) yields

−𝑖ℏ𝑆″+𝑆′2=𝑝2(𝑥). (20)

We will argue that ℏ𝑆″ is small for slowly varying potentials, justifying taking ℏ to be the small parameter in a power series expansion of 𝑆(𝑥):

𝑆(𝑥)=𝑆0(𝑥)+ℏ𝑆1(𝑥)+ℏ2𝑆2(𝑥)+⋯. (21)

Plugging this into the Equation 20 yields

(𝑆0′)2+ℏ(2𝑆0′𝑆1′−𝑖𝑆0″)+𝑂(ℏ)2=𝑝2(𝑥). (22)

Matching terms by order of ℏ yields (𝑆0′)2−𝑝2(𝑥)=0 and 2𝑆0′𝑆1′−𝑖𝑆0″=0. We get

𝑆0(𝑥)=±∫𝑥0𝑥𝑝(𝑥′)d𝑥′,𝑖𝑆1(𝑥)=−12log𝑝(𝑥)+𝐶 (23)

The basic solution in the WKB approximation is thus

𝜓(𝑥)=exp(𝑖ℏ(𝑆0+ℏ𝑆1+𝑂(ℏ2)))=exp(𝑖ℏ𝑆0)exp(𝑖𝑆1)exp(𝑖ℏ𝑂(ℏ2))≈𝐴𝑝(𝑥)exp(±𝑖ℏ∫𝑥0𝑥𝑝(𝑥′)d𝑥). (24)

Our general solutions thus look like 𝐴exp(+…)+𝐵exp(−…) in the classical region and 𝐶cos(…)+𝐷sin(…) in the forbidden region.

    • 𝐽=𝜌𝑚𝜕𝑆𝜕𝑥=|𝐴|2/𝑚, which is constant as expected since 𝜕𝜌𝜕𝑡=0.

    This yields |d𝜆d𝑥|≪1, or equivalently

𝜓tr=𝐹𝑘(𝑥)cos(…)+𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑥)sin(…). (35)

𝑇=|𝐹|2|−𝑖𝐹𝑒𝜃|2=exp(−2ℏ∫𝑏𝑐d𝑥2𝑚𝑉(𝑥)−𝐸) (37)
