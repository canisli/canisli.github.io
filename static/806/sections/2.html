2. Time-Independent Perturbation Theory (TIPT)

2.1. Nondegenerate PT

Idea: Suppose we can model 𝐻=𝐻(0)+𝛿𝐻 where 𝛿𝐻is “small” compared to 𝐻(0). It is useful to add a dimensionless parameter 𝜆∈[0,1] so that 𝐻(𝜆)=𝐻(0)+𝜆𝛿𝐻. We then expect to be able to use a power series expansion to find energy eigenvalues and eigenstates as a function of 𝜆. Since 𝐻(0) and 𝛿𝐻 operate on the same Hilbert space, by a continuity argument we expect eigenstates to not be created/destroyed.

Nondegenerate PT: Suppose the 𝑛th energy level is nondegenerate. We will write the perturbed eigenvalue and eigenvector as a power series in 𝜆 (Rayleigh–Schrödinger):

𝐸𝑛(𝜆)=𝐸𝑛(0)+𝜆𝐸𝑛(1)+𝜆2𝐸𝑛(2)+…|𝑛⟩𝜆=|𝑛(0)⟩+𝜆|𝑛(1)⟩+𝜆2|𝑛(2)⟩+… (1)

The perturbed state must satisfy

(𝐻(0)+𝜆𝛿𝐻−𝐸𝑛(𝜆))|𝑛⟩𝜆=0. (2)

This occurs if and only if each term of order 𝜆𝑘 equals zero for all 𝑘. Considering each order in Equation 2 yields

𝜆0:(𝐻(0)−𝐸𝑛(0))|𝑛(0)⟩=0𝜆1:(𝐻(0)−𝐸𝑛(0))|𝑛(1)⟩=(𝐸𝑛(1)−𝛿𝐻)|𝑛(0)⟩𝜆2:(𝐻(0)−𝐸𝑛(0))|𝑛(2)⟩=(𝐸𝑛(1)−𝛿𝐻)|𝑛(1)⟩+𝐸𝑛(2)|𝑛(0)⟩⋮𝜆𝑘:(𝐻(0)−𝐸𝑛(0))|𝑛(𝑘)⟩=(𝐸𝑛(1)−𝛿𝐻)|𝑛(𝑘−1)⟩+𝐸𝑛(2)|𝑛(𝑘−2)⟩+…+𝐸𝑛(𝑘)|𝑛(0)⟩. (3)

The following proposition makes our lives much easier.

Proposition 1. We can choose perturbation terms |𝑛(𝑘)⟩ such that ⟨𝑛(0)|𝑛(𝑘)⟩=0 for 𝑘≥1.
Proof

Let |𝑛(𝑘)⟩=|𝑛(𝑘)⟩′−𝑎𝑘|𝑛(0)⟩ with 𝑎𝑘=−⟨𝑛(𝑘)|𝑛(0)⟩. Substitute these into |𝑛⟩𝜆 to obtain

|𝑛⟩𝜆=(1−𝑎1𝜆−𝑎2𝜆2−⋯)|𝑛(0)⟩+𝜆|𝑛(1)⟩′+𝜆2+|𝑛(2)⟩′+⋯. (4)
Dividing this by (1−𝑎1𝜆−𝑎2𝜆2−⋯) yields a solution |𝑛̃⟩𝜆 that is a perturbation of |𝑛(0)⟩ and satisfies the hypothesis.

□

Assume from hereon the perturbation terms satisfy the above claim. We can take the inner product of the 𝜆𝑗 equation with ⟨𝑛(0)| to conclude that 0=⟨𝑛(0)|−𝛿𝐻|𝑛(𝑗−1)⟩+𝐸𝑛(𝑗), yielding the result

⭐️𝐸𝑛(𝑗)=⟨𝑛(0)|𝛿𝐻|𝑛(𝑗−1)⟩. (5)

To compute the first order correction to the eigenstate, take the inner product of the 𝜆1 equation with ⟨𝑘(0)| for the other energy eigenstates |𝑘≠𝑛⟩ to conclude that

(𝐸𝑘(0)−𝐸𝑛(0))⟨𝑘(0)|𝑛(1)⟩=−⟨𝑘(0)|𝛿𝐻|𝑛(0)⟩⟹⭐️|𝑛(1)⟩=−∑𝑘≠𝑛𝛿𝐻𝑘𝑛𝐸𝑘(0)−𝐸𝑛(0)|𝑘(0)⟩,⭐️𝐸𝑛(2)=−∑𝑘≠𝑛|𝛿𝐻𝑘𝑛|2𝐸𝑘(0)−𝐸𝑛(0) (6)

The summation skips 𝑛 because of Proposition 1.

Remarks

  • For the ground state, the first order energy is always larger than the actual ground state since the second order term is nonpositive, but more generally regardless of the magnitude of 𝜆. Proof comes from variational principle:

    𝐸1=⟨𝜓0|𝐻+𝑉|𝜓0|𝜓0|𝐻+𝑉|𝜓0⟩≥𝐸 (7)
  • The second order correction is called “level-level repulsion”, since the levels below/above have a positive/negative contribution to 𝐸𝑛(2) respectively.
  • When is this method valid? We want to study the conditions for the Taylor series in 𝜆 to converge. Zwiebach explores the convergence of a simple 2×2 example using complex analysis. In general we want 𝜆|𝛿𝐻𝑘𝑛|/|𝐸𝑘0−𝐸𝑛0|≪1, i.e. elements of 𝜆𝛿𝐻 are much smaller than energy eigenvalue spacings, for perturbation theory to converge quickly.

The Rayleigh-Schrodinger expansion is applicable up to level crossings, where the Taylor Series stops converging. For example, see https://chatgpt.com/c/6806fe04-0744-800d-98ab-486f622353a1.

Intuition: Start a perburabtion at the hamiltonian where the level crossing starts. There are we sum over terms with 𝐸𝑘−𝐸𝑛 in the denominator for the second order energy correction, and these will blow up. It’s the exact same reason why you can’t apply PT naively to degenerate case.

2.2. Degenerate PT

If the 𝑛th energy level is degenerate, the choice of eigenstates is ambiguous, and hence we are tasked with selecting a “good” basis so the eigenvectors do not jump discontinuously.

Suppose the eigenspace 𝑉𝑛 of 𝐸𝑛(0) has degeneracy 𝑁. Start with an arbitrary “bad” basis

{|𝑛(0),1⟩,|𝑛(0),2⟩,…,|𝑛(0),𝑁⟩}. (8)

How do we find a basis in which the series does not diverge?

Theorem 1.
  1. The good states are the eigenvectors of [𝛿𝐻], the restriction of 𝛿𝐻 to 𝑉𝑛.
  2. The corresponding first order energy corrections are the the respective eigenvalues of [𝛿𝐻].
Proof
  1. If there are any off-diagonal terms in [𝛿𝐻], then the formulas above from first order perturbation theory blow up, as we get division by zero.
  2. Follows from (1).

□

Idea 1. The matrix [𝛿𝐻] is diagonal for a choice of basis in 𝕍𝑁 if for any two different basis vectors there is a Hermitian operator 𝐾 that commutes with 𝛿𝐻 for which the two basis vectors are 𝐾 eigenstates with different eigenvlaues.

After finding the good basis states, we can proceed in the same manner to compute the second order corrections to the energy.

2.3. Example: Fine Structure

Solving the hydrogen atom relativistically with the Dirac equation yields some more leading order terms:

𝐻=𝐩22𝑚+𝑉(𝑟)−𝐩48𝑚3𝑐2⏟𝛿𝐻rel+12𝑚2𝑐21𝑟d𝑉d𝑟(𝐒⋅𝐋)⏟𝛿𝐻spin-orbit+ℏ28𝑚2𝑐2∇2𝑉(𝑟)⏟𝛿𝐻Darwin (9)
  • The first two have completely classical interpretations. Viewed in the reference frame of the electron , we need to correct by changing reference frames (Thomas precession).
  • The Darwin term comes from the fact that the electron cannot be treated as a point particle. Rather it is a fuzzy blob of size 𝜆𝐶=ℏ𝑚𝑒𝑐. Thus we should replace 𝑉(𝑟) with its ⟨𝑉(𝑟)⟩. See iPad notes for mathematical treatment. Note that since ∇2𝑉∝𝛿3(𝐫), this correction only affects 𝑙=0 wavefunctions (nonzero at the orgin).
  • The hydrogen energy is of order 𝛼2𝑚𝑐2. The three correction terms are all of order 𝛼4𝑚𝑐2. Thus we can absorb 𝜆=𝛼2 into the three perturbation terms.
    • Mathematical trick: ⟨𝑝4⟩=⟨𝐩2𝜓|𝐩2𝜓⟩ where 𝐩2=2𝑚(𝐸−𝑉).
  • The uncoupled basis is a good basis for 𝛿𝐻rel and 𝛿𝐻D but not 𝛿𝐻spin-orbit, as neither 𝐋 or 𝐒 commute with 𝐋⋅𝐒. The coupled basis is a good basis for all three.

Theorem 2 (Fine Structure). The first order energy corrections from fine structure are given by

𝐸𝑛𝑙𝑗𝑚𝑗(1)fs={𝐸(1)rel+𝐸(1)SO𝑙>0𝐸(1)rel+𝐸(1)D𝑙=0=𝛼2𝐸𝑛(0)𝑛2(𝑛𝑗+12−34) (10)

Remarks

  • Magically, the total fine structure shift (even including higher order energy corrections) does not depend on 𝑙. Fact relies on trying 𝑙=𝑗±12 (this expression comes from addition of angular momentum) which both yield the same answer
  • All the energy shifts are negative (same sign as 𝐸𝑛(0)), since 𝑛𝑗max+12=𝑛𝑛≥34.
  • Label the coupled basis multiplets 𝑛𝐿𝑗 (spectroscopic notation). There is still degeneracy between 2𝑆1/2 and 2𝑃1/2. The Lamb shift, which is of order 5, resolves these.
  • Hyperfine splitting, caused by the coupling of the electron and proton magnetic moments, causes splitting that is a factor 𝑚𝑒/𝑚𝑝 smaller than fine structure.
