Definition 37. A subfield 𝐹⊂𝐾 is a field under the same addition and multiplication operations as 𝐾. Equivalently, 𝐹 is a unital (shares multiplicative identity) subring of 𝐾 that is closed under taking inverses of nonzero elements
Proposition 40. Any subfield of ℂ contains ℚ.
Proof
A subfield of ℂ inherits the zero, identity, and operations of ℂ. Thus any subfield contains 1 and thus ℚ.

□

The three most important classes of fields are

  1. Number fields (subfield of ℂ).
  2. Finite fields
  3. Function fields

6.1. Algebraic, transcendental elements, extension fields

Definition 38. Let 𝐹 and 𝐾 be fields. When 𝐹⊂𝐾, say 𝐾 is an extension field of 𝐹. 𝐾 forms a vector space over 𝐹, and we define the [𝐾:𝐹] of 𝐾 over 𝐹 as dim𝐹𝐾.

Examples.

  1. ℚ[𝑑] for 𝑑∈ℤ square free is an extension field with [𝐸:ℚ]=2
  2. [ℂ:ℝ]=2.
  3. [ℚ[2,3]:ℚ]=4. A basis is {1,2,3,6}.
  4. [ℝ:ℚ]=∞.

How can we construct an extension field 𝐸 of 𝐹? Take irreducible 𝑓(𝑥)∈𝐹[𝑥], and let 𝐸=𝐹[𝑥]/(𝑓)≅𝐹(𝛼) where 𝛼 is the image of 𝑥 in 𝐹[𝑥]/(𝑓). For example, ℚ(23)≅ℚ[𝑥]/(𝑥3−2)≅ℚ[23]. Note that ℚ(23)=ℚ[23].

Notation: 𝐹[𝛼] is the smallest ring containing 𝐹 and 𝛼. 𝐹(𝛼)is the smallest field containing 𝐹 and 𝛼. The first consists of polynomials in 𝛼 with ratoinal coefficients while the latter consists of rational functions. They are equal if and only if 𝛼 is algebraic (Proposition 41)

Definition 39. Let 𝐸⊃𝐹. We say 𝛼∈𝐸 is algebraic if it is a root of an irreducible monic polynomial with coefficients in 𝐹. Otherwise, 𝛼 is transcendental over 𝐹.

This collapses to our familiar definition of algebraic numbers when 𝐸=ℂ and 𝐹=ℚ.

Proposition 41.
  1. 𝛼∈𝐸 is algebraic if and only if [𝐹[𝛼]:𝐹] is finite.
  2. 𝛼∈𝐸 is algebraic if and only if 𝐹(𝛼)=𝐹[𝛼], i.e. 𝐹[𝛼] is a field.
  3. If 𝛼 is transcendental over 𝐹, then 𝐹[𝛼]≅𝐹[𝑥] as a ring.
Proof
  1. If 𝛼 is algebraic, then its degree over 𝐹 equals the degree of its irreducible polynomial 𝑓over 𝐹. If [𝐹[𝛼]:𝐹] is finite, then the powers {1,𝛼,𝛼2,…,𝛼𝑛} have a linear independence relation.
  2. Since 𝐹[𝑥] is a PID, we have 𝑓 is irreducible if and only if (𝑓) is maximal, if and only if 𝐹[𝑥]/(𝑓) is a field.
  3. If 𝛼 is transcendental, the surjective evaluation map 𝜑:𝐹[𝑥]→𝐹[𝛼] is injective, and hence is an isomorphism.

□

If 𝛼is algebraic over 𝐹 with irreducible polynomial 𝑓, then (1,𝛼,…,𝛼𝑛−1) where 𝑛 is 𝑓 is a basis for 𝐹(𝛼) as a vector space over 𝐹. Thus a necessary condition for two algebraic elements to generate isomorphic field extensions is that they have the same degree, since all bases of a vector space have the same length. This is clearly not sufficient, as all quadratic number fields have degree 2 over ℚ.

Here’s a cool case where ℚ(𝛼)=ℚ(𝛽). Let 𝛼 be a complex root of 𝑥3−𝑥+1 and 𝛽=𝛼+1 be a root of 𝑥3−3𝑥2+2𝑥+1. This is hard to see if we were only given the polynomials.

We will focus 𝐹-isomorphisms: isomorphisms 𝐹(𝛼)→𝐹(𝛽) that fix 𝐹 and send 𝛼 to 𝛽. The next proposition will gives a clear condition.

Proposition 42 (Artin 15.2.8 — Conditions for F-isomorphism). Let 𝐹 be a field, 𝐾/𝐹 and 𝐿/𝐹 be two field extensions, and 𝛼∈𝐾,𝛽∈𝐿. Suppose 𝛼,𝛽 are algebraic over 𝐹. There exists an 𝐹-isomorphism 𝜎:𝐹(𝛼)→𝐹(𝛽) mapping 𝛼⇝𝛽 if and only if 𝛼 and 𝛽 have the same irreducible polynomials over 𝐹.
Proof
Consider the isomorphisms 𝜑 and 𝜓 granted by between 𝐹[𝑥]/(𝑓) and 𝐹[𝛼],𝐹[𝛽] respectively. 𝜎=𝜓𝜑−1 is the required isomorphism 𝐹(𝛼)→𝐹(𝛽). Conversely, if such isomorphism exists then 𝜎 preserves roots so they must have the same irreducible polynomial.

□

The isomorphism is not unique. Consider 𝐺(ℚ(2,3)/𝑄)=𝐶2×𝐶2.

6.2. Finding the irreducible polynomials

Suppose 𝛾∈𝐸⊃𝐹 is algebraic over 𝐹. How can you find an irreducible polynomial 𝑓 of 𝛾?

  • Method 1. Bash a linear dependence relation between powers of 𝛾.
  • Method 2. Use the fact that any 𝐹-automorphism maps roots of 𝑓𝛾 to roots of 𝑓𝛾 and guess what the automorphism might look likei. Suppose 𝛾=2+3. Then the possible roots are ±2±3. Multiple the product of these (𝑥−𝑟𝑖) terms to get 𝑥4−10𝑥2+1.

This example shows that ℚ(2+3)=ℚ(2,3), which doesn’t seem obvious immediately. We have 2=12(𝛾3−9𝛾2) and then we can extract 3=𝛾−2. This is a preview of the primitive element theorem.

Let 𝑓 be an irreducible polynomial of 𝐹. We can construct an extension 𝐸=𝐹[𝑥]/(𝑓(𝑥))=𝐹[𝛼]. Let 𝛼 be the image of 𝑥. Then 𝛼 is a root of 𝑥. Irreduciblity is required so that (𝑓(𝑥)) is maximal and 𝐸 is a field.
